How to use Second Opinion

A quick tour of what the app measures and how to use it as one input in your IM SAFE and ADM workflow.

Big picture

Second Opinion gives you a structured, repeatable way to check three parts of your current mental “sharpness” before you go fly:

  • Raw reaction time
  • Executive control (Stroop task)
  • Working memory (2-back)

The app compares today’s results to your personal baseline so that you’re looking at your normal, not someone else’s.

How often to use it

  • Run several “baseline” sessions on days you feel well-rested and healthy.
  • Before flights where you’re PIC, run a quick check if time and context allow.
  • Treat the output as decision support, not a verdict. You are always the final authority.

How to read the results

After each test, you’ll see today’s scores alongside your historical baseline. If today is significantly worse than your typical range, that’s a prompt to slow down and reassess:

  • Is fatigue higher than you admitted on IM SAFE?
  • Are you distracted, stressed, or under pressure to complete this trip?
  • Would delaying, taking another pilot, or changing plans improve safety?

None of this replaces your CFI, the regulations, or your own judgement. It simply gives you more data about today’s brain state.

About the app

What Second Opinion is

Second Opinion is a lightweight pre-flight check for your current cognitive sharpness and risk picture. It’s built by pilots for pilots and is intended to live alongside your existing tools: IM SAFE, PAVE, FRATs, checklists, and good ADM.

The cognitive tasks are drawn from decades of research on fatigue, attention, executive function, and working memory — repackaged in a form that fits into real pre-flight workflows.

What it is not

  • Not a medical device
  • Not a substitute for regulatory requirements or your CFI
  • Not a guarantee you are “safe to fly”

Second Opinion is one more nudge toward thoughtful, conservative aeronautical decision making.

Cognition & Acuity Assessment

A short sequence of tasks that samples your reaction time, executive control, and working memory, then compares today to your baseline.

What you’ll do:

  1. Simple reaction-time taps
  2. Color-word (Stroop) decisions
  3. Short 2-back working-memory task

You’ll get a concise summary showing how today compares to your prior “good-day” baseline and whether today looks typical, somewhat degraded, or significantly worse.

What the assessment measures

Reaction time

The simple reaction-time (SRT) segment measures how quickly you respond when a stimulus appears. Longer-than-usual reactions can reflect fatigue, distraction, or low arousal.

Executive control

The Stroop task asks you to respond to ink color even when the printed word conflicts. It’s a classic probe of executive function — your ability to inhibit a pre-potent response and follow the rule instead.

Working memory

The 2-back segment requires you to hold recent items in mind and constantly update. It’s a quick read on working-memory capacity and focus.
